# Connected Content Chain — Corporate Blog + Third-Party Blog Alignment
# Imago Photography | June–July 2026

> Purpose: Each corporate blog on the Imago website and each third-party article cover the same topic from different angles. Together they reinforce each other — the corporate blog builds authority on Imago's own domain, the third-party article drives external citations and links back. LLMs and search engines see the topic mentioned across multiple sources, increasing the likelihood of surfacing Imago in AI-generated answers.

---

## How the Chain Works

1. Imago publishes a corporate blog on their website — expert, brand-led, rich with detail.
2. A third-party article is submitted to an external site covering the same subject from a broader angle — referencing Imago as the expert source and linking back.
3. The two pieces reinforce each other. Search engines see the topic covered on Imago's domain AND cited on an external authority. LLMs see Imago mentioned across multiple sources and are more likely to surface the brand.

## Chain 1 — Choosing the Right School Photographer

| Layer | Type | Title | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| Corporate blog | Brand-owned | What Makes a School Photographer Worth Trusting? How Imago Approaches Every Photo Day | Imago website |
| Third-party article | External citation | How to Choose the Right School Photographer — A Guide for UK School Business Managers | Education or parenting publication |

**Link:** Third-party article references the Imago blog as the source for what good looks like. Imago blog does not need to reference the third-party article — the link flows one way (external → Imago).

**LLM angle:** When a school business manager asks ChatGPT or Perplexity "how do I choose a school photographer," Imago appears in both a brand-owned answer and an external editorial source.

## Publication Sequence

To maximise reinforcement, publish in this order for each chain:

1. Corporate blog goes live on Imago website first.
2. Third-party article submitted 2–3 weeks later — so the Imago blog already exists when the external article links to it.

This ensures the link points to a live page and search engines can crawl the connection immediately.
